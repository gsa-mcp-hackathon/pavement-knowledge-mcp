# Heavy-load experiment comparison

Pavement Knowledge MCP team. Assessment: November 10, 2026. Project-team pavement SME approval: November 12, 2026.

## Question and finding

The task compared the sloped-shoulder and buried-utility experiments in FHWA-HRT-18-050: pavement setup, loading, measured responses and supporting pages or tables. It also asked what these experiments support for a transporter carrying a 3-million-pound transformer and what further validation would be needed.

Without pavement MCP, the assistant gave useful general guidance and avoided inventing report details it could not verify. With MCP, it supplied the experimental comparison and source locations. Both answers offered engineering reasoning and broadly similar follow-up testing. The main benefit was access to report-specific evidence.

## Source checks and limits

Source: [FHWA-HRT-18-050](https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/publications/research/infrastructure/pavements/18050/18050.pdf). Locations below use PDF pages, including front matter and errata.

| Topic | Review finding and location |
|---|---|
| Shoulder setup and loading | Dimensions, slope, load levels, pulse timing, cycles and offsets agree with Table 13, PDF 65 (printed 52). Static loading and sensor descriptions agree with PDF 74. |
| Buried utilities | Dimensions, placement, loading and instrumentation agree with PDF 75 and 83–89, including Table 15. |
| Detailed experimental results | The report directs readers to Volumes VII and VIII; see PDF 90 and 108. |
| Two versus three loading locations | Unresolved. Table 1 (PDF 14), Figure 73 (PDF 77) and instrumentation text (PDF 86) describe three locations. The detailed protocol (PDF 85) describes two loading phases above the pipe and culvert. The evidence does not establish whether a third position was loaded. |

Tables 13 and 15 and the utility setup figure were visually checked during the source review. The answer correctly flags the loading-location conflict, but its wording is incomplete if read as saying that only the introduction mentions three locations.

The 77-inch laboratory section includes subgrade; it is not 77 inches of asphalt. These records describe test setup and measured quantities. Applying them to the proposed transporter and designing follow-up tests require engineering judgment; they do not establish route or transporter approval.
